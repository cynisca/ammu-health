Critical Federal Law Change (January 2026)

The “One Big Beautiful Bill Act” (OBBBA), signed into law in summer 2025, eliminated ACA marketplace subsidies for lawfully present immigrants with income below 100% of the federal poverty level ($15,650/year) who are ineligible for Medicaid.

Previously (2014–2025), this group could receive premium tax credits that made marketplace coverage nearly free. That exception no longer exists as of January 1, 2026.

Without subsidies, an unsubsidized marketplace silver plan for a 65-year-old costs approximately $1,300–$1,450/month. State-level subsidy programs may help—see details below.

Key question to ask Covered California or Washington Healthplanfinder: “Does the state premium subsidy program cover a lawful permanent resident with zero income who lost federal ACA subsidies under the OBBBA?”

Key Takeaway

Neither state is clearly better right now. The federal OBBBA change in 2026 created a coverage gap in both states for new green card holders with zero income. The critical next step is calling both state marketplaces to ask about state-funded subsidies:

• Covered California: (800) 300-1506 — Ask if the state subsidy covers below-FPL LPRs who lost federal subsidies
• WA Healthplanfinder: (855) 923-4633 — Ask about Cascade Care Savings for this situation

If even modest income (~$15,650/year) is possible through part-time work, federal ACA subsidies become available again in either state, and premiums would drop to under $50/month.

Washington-Specific Notes

  • Apple Health (WA Medicaid): The standard federal 5-year bar applies. Unlike California (which previously waived it), Washington has always required the 5-year wait for LPRs.
  • Apple Health Expansion: A state-funded Medicaid-like program launched July 2024 but enrollment is closed (capped at ~13,000). It also does not cover LPRs in the 5-year bar.
  • Cascade Care Savings: State-funded subsidy of $250/month per member for those ineligible for federal subsidies with income ≤250% FPL. This is a known, available benefit.
  • 1332 Waiver: Through 2028, Washington allows all residents to purchase marketplace plans regardless of immigration status. This may provide better long-term protection than California.
  • Alien Emergency Medical: WA’s equivalent of Emergency Medi-Cal. Covers emergency conditions.
Marketplace Coverage

ACA Marketplace (Covered California / WA Healthplanfinder)

The marketplace is still the primary path to comprehensive coverage, but affordability depends on subsidies.

Eligibility

  • As a lawful permanent resident (green card holder), she is eligible to enroll in marketplace plans with no waiting period in both CA and WA.
  • Sponsor deeming does not apply. Unlike means-tested programs (Medicaid, SNAP), ACA marketplace subsidies do not count the sponsor’s income. Only the applicant’s own income matters. See the Sponsor Rules section.

The Subsidy Problem (OBBBA Change)

Federal subsidies are no longer available for this situation. Before 2026, lawfully present immigrants below 100% FPL who were ineligible for Medicaid could receive premium tax credits (often resulting in $0 premiums). The “One Big Beautiful Bill Act” eliminated this exception effective January 1, 2026.
  • With zero income (below 100% FPL of $15,650/year), she cannot receive federal premium tax credits.
  • Without subsidies, a silver plan for a 65-year-old costs approximately $1,300–$1,450/month depending on location.
  • If income reaches $15,650/year (100% FPL) through any work, federal subsidies become available again—premiums could drop to under $50/month for a silver plan.

Future Option

Medicare — Not Yet Eligible

Medicare is the long-term goal, but there are barriers for new permanent residents.

Why She Can't Enroll Yet

  • Medicare requires 5 years of continuous US residency before a permanent resident can enroll.
  • Without 40 quarters (~10 years) of US work history, Part A (hospital insurance) is not free.
  • After 5 years, she can buy Part A at approximately $518/month (2025 rate) plus Part B (doctors) at $185/month.
Spouse exception: If her spouse worked 10+ years in the US and paid Medicare taxes, she may qualify for premium-free Part A through her spouse’s work record. Worth checking!

Programs That Can Help Pay (After 5 Years)

Once she becomes Medicare-eligible, several programs can cover the costs for people with low income:

QMB
Qualified Medicare Beneficiary — Pays all Medicare premiums, deductibles, and copays. Income must be at or below 100% FPL (~$15,060/year). Asset limit: $9,660.
SLMB
Specified Low-Income Medicare Beneficiary — Covers Part B premium. Income up to 135% FPL.
QI
Qualifying Individual — Covers Part B premium. Income up to 150% FPL.
Extra Help
Part D Low-Income Subsidy — Reduces prescription drug costs (premiums, deductibles, copays).
With zero income, she would very likely qualify for QMB, which would cover essentially all Medicare costs. This makes Medicare affordable once the 5-year residency requirement is met.
Important Changes

Medicaid (Medi-Cal / Apple Health)

Both states have restrictions that prevent new green card holders from accessing full Medicaid.

California: What Changed on January 1, 2026

  • Previously, California had no 5-year waiting period for permanent residents—full-scope Medi-Cal was available immediately.
  • As of January 1, 2026, California enacted an enrollment freeze for new applicants with “unsatisfactory immigration status.”
  • Green card holders with less than 5 years of residency are now classified under this category.
  • New applicants in this category can only receive Emergency Medi-Cal.
This is a significant change. If she had applied before January 1, 2026, she could have received full-scope coverage. Those already enrolled keep their benefits. Only new applicants are affected by the freeze.

Washington: Apple Health (Medicaid)

  • The standard federal 5-year bar applies. Washington has never waived it for LPRs (unlike California, which previously did).
  • Apple Health Expansion (state-funded Medicaid-like program) launched July 2024 but enrollment is closed (capped at ~13,000 people). It also does not cover LPRs in the 5-year bar.
  • Alien Emergency Medical is available for emergency conditions, similar to CA’s Emergency Medi-Cal.
  • After 5 years, the applicant can qualify for full Apple Health. Income limit for seniors: ~$994/month (SSI-related).

Income & Asset Limits (If She Qualifies Later)

Income Limit
$1,801/month for an individual (she qualifies with no income)
Asset Limit
$130,000 for an individual (reinstated Jan 2026)

Important Exception to Check

If the applicant came to the US as a refugee or asylee (rather than through a regular family-sponsored green card), they may be exempt from the 5-year bar and could still qualify for full-scope Medi-Cal. Check the exact immigration category on the I-94 or green card documentation.

Other exempt categories include: Cuban/Haitian entrants, victims of trafficking, certain Amerasian immigrants, and veterans or active-duty military.

Sponsor Deeming & Medi-Cal

Even once the 5-year bar is satisfied, Medi-Cal eligibility can be affected by sponsor deeming—the sponsor’s income and assets are counted as the applicant’s. If the sponsor earns above the Medi-Cal income limit, the applicant may be found ineligible. See the Affidavit of Support section below for full details and exemptions.

Safety Net

Emergency Medicaid (CA & WA)

Available right now in both states as a safety net, regardless of immigration status or waiting periods.

What It Covers

  • Emergency room visits
  • Emergency surgeries
  • Emergency medical conditions (heart attacks, strokes, severe injuries, etc.)
  • Labor and delivery

This does not cover routine doctor visits, prescriptions, or preventive care—which is why marketplace coverage and FQHCs are critical for day-to-day health needs.

Legal Obligation

Affidavit of Support (Form I-864)

The person who sponsored the green card signed a legally binding financial contract. A presidential memorandum directs federal agencies to aggressively enforce these obligations.

What Is Sponsor Deeming?

When a sponsored immigrant applies for means-tested public benefits (Medi-Cal, SNAP, TANF), the government “deems” the sponsor’s income and assets as the immigrant’s own for eligibility purposes. This means:

  • Even if the immigrant has zero personal income, the sponsor’s earnings are counted when determining benefit eligibility.
  • If the sponsor earns above the program’s income limit, the immigrant may be found ineligible for the benefit.
  • This deeming lasts until the immigrant naturalizes (becomes a US citizen) or is credited with 40 qualifying quarters of work.

Sponsor Reimbursement Liability

If a sponsored immigrant receives means-tested benefits, the government can pursue the sponsor for reimbursement of those costs:

  • Federal agencies are directed to use the Treasury Offset Program for debt collection from sponsors.
  • Non-paying sponsors can be referred to the Attorney General for enforcement.
  • California has up to 10 years to pursue repayment claims.
Important context: California has historically not pursued sponsor reimbursement claims. However, the current federal administration is actively pushing states to enforce these provisions.

What Is and Isn’t Affected

Not Affected
Covered California / ACA marketplace plans. These are not means-tested benefits. Sponsor deeming does not apply. Only the applicant’s own income is used for subsidy calculations.
Not Affected
Emergency Medi-Cal. Explicitly exempt from sponsor deeming and reimbursement requirements.
Not Affected
Medicare. Not a means-tested benefit. Sponsor deeming does not apply to eligibility.
Affected
Full-scope Medi-Cal. Sponsor’s income is deemed to the applicant. Could make the applicant ineligible even with zero personal income.
Affected
SNAP (CalFresh) and CalWORKs (TANF). Sponsor deeming applies to these food and cash assistance programs as well.

Exemptions from Sponsor Deeming

Deeming may be waived in these situations:

  • Indigence exemption: If the immigrant can demonstrate they would go hungry or homeless without benefits, deeming can be waived for 12 months at a time (though the sponsor may still owe reimbursement).
  • Domestic violence: Victims of battery or extreme cruelty by the sponsor or sponsor’s spouse are exempt.
  • Sponsor is deceased or disabled.
  • Refugees and asylees are not subject to sponsor deeming (they typically don’t have I-864 sponsors).

Bottom Line

Sponsor deeming does not affect ACA marketplace plans or Emergency Medicaid. Since marketplace plans are not means-tested, the sponsor faces no liability for marketplace enrollment, and the applicant’s eligibility is based solely on their own income. However, note that with zero income, federal subsidies are no longer available (see Federal Alert)—state subsidies and FQHCs are the best paths forward. Means-tested benefits (full-scope Medicaid, SNAP) are affected by deeming and may trigger sponsor reimbursement.
What To Do

Action Plan & Timeline

A step-by-step plan covering what to do now, what to prepare for, and long-term milestones.

Now — February 2026
Find a Federally Qualified Health Center (FQHC).
This is the most important first step. FQHCs offer doctor visits, prescriptions, and preventive care on a sliding-scale fee regardless of insurance or immigration status.
Call the ACA marketplace to ask about state subsidies.
CA: Covered California at (800) 300-1506 — ask if the state subsidy program covers below-FPL LPRs who lost federal subsidies under OBBBA.
WA: Healthplanfinder at (855) 923-4633 — ask about Cascade Care Savings ($250/month subsidy).
Apply for Emergency Medicaid through your county office (CA) or HCA (WA) as a safety net for emergencies.
Confirm the immigration category. Check the I-94, visa stamp, or green card. If entry was as a refugee or asylee, full-scope Medicaid may be available immediately.
Explore whether any income (~$15,650/year) is possible. Even modest part-time work restores federal ACA subsidies, dropping marketplace premiums to under $50/month.
Understand the sponsor’s I-864 obligations. Review the Affidavit of Support section. ACA marketplace plans are not affected by sponsor deeming, but means-tested benefits (Medicaid, SNAP) are. Consider consulting a free legal aid organization.
Within 5 Years
Track the 5-year residency date carefully. Mark the date of permanent residency—that’s when the clock starts for Medicare and full Medicaid eligibility.
Monitor Medicaid policy changes in both states. CA’s enrollment freeze could be reversed by future legislation. WA’s Apple Health Expansion could reopen or expand eligibility.
If possible, accumulate Social Security credits. 40 quarters (about 10 years of work) = premium-free Medicare Part A. Even part-time work counts.
After 5 Years (~2031)
Enroll in Medicare. She’ll need to pay premiums without sufficient work history, but assistance programs can help.
Apply for the QMB program to cover all Medicare costs if her income stays below 100% of the federal poverty level.
Apply for full-scope Medicaid. The 5-year bar will be satisfied in both states. Note: sponsor deeming may still apply—the sponsor’s income could affect eligibility unless an exemption (indigence, naturalization, or 40 work quarters) is met.
